    <p>I am a PhD student in <strong>Economic History</strong> at the <strong>London School of Economics and Political Science (LSE)</strong>. I began my PhD in September 2024 and am supervised by <a href="https://www.lse.ac.uk/people/patrick-wallis">Prof. Patrick Wallis</a> and <a href="https://www.melaniexue.net/">Dr Melanie Meng Xue</a>.</p>

    <p>My research interests lie at the intersection of economic history, welfare, and public economics. I am particularly interested in public decision-making, individual motivations and behaviour (including altruism), institutions, and their long-term evolution.</p>

    <p>My PhD dissertation examines the evolution of poor relief and private charity in England, focusing on 1600–1786. It asks why England developed a locally administered, publicly financed system of poor relief that expanded over more than 200 years; how the allocation of assistance became increasingly selective in recipients and purposes as provision grew; and how public relief and private charity interacted over time.</p>
