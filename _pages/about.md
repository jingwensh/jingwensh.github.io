I am a PhD student in **Economic History** at the **London School of Economics and Political Science (LSE)**, supervised by **Patrick Wallis** and **Melanie Meng Xue**.

My research focuses on welfare, poverty, and economic change in early modern England. I am particularly interested in how local institutions financed and allocated assistance, how parish poor relief responded to subsistence pressures, and the relationship between public relief, private charity, and labour markets.

My doctoral research examines the development of poor relief and charity in England, including the expansion of parish relief between 1600 and 1786 and the possible labour-market functions of assistance before Speenhamland. The work combines historical sources with quantitative analysis.

You can read more about my [research]({{ '/research/' | relative_url }}), [teaching]({{ '/teaching/' | relative_url }}), and [academic CV]({{ '/cv/' | relative_url }}).

### Research interests

Economic history · Poverty and welfare · Labour markets · Historical institutions · Early modern England

### Current work

- **Parish poor relief and subsistence shocks.** Understanding the expansion and responses of English parish relief across the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries.
- **Poor relief and wages before Speenhamland.** Investigating whether relief was more responsive to food-price shocks when wages were weak, and what that implies for interpretations of wage supplementation.
- **Private charity and parish relief.** Comparing how different forms of assistance were financed, targeted, and allocated.
