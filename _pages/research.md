My research examines poverty, welfare provision, and local institutions in early modern England. A central question is how communities responded to economic pressure through parish poor relief and private charity.

The projects below are **works in progress**. Their titles and descriptions may change as the research develops; papers are not posted for download unless a public version is ready.

## Why Did Poor Relief Expand? Parish Relief in England, 1600–1786

*Work in progress*

This project investigates the long-run expansion of parish poor relief in England. Using a panel of parish-level relief records, it studies the relationship between subsistence pressure and relief provision, as well as the ways local fiscal and administrative circumstances may have shaped these responses.

## Did Poor Relief Supplement Wages before Speenhamland?

*Work in progress*

This paper asks whether English parish relief functioned in part as wage supplementation before the better-known late-eighteenth-century Speenhamland arrangements. It examines how relief responded to food-price changes under differing wage conditions. The analysis distinguishes a general response to subsistence stress from the more specific patterns that a wage-supplementation interpretation would require.

## Private Charity and Parish Relief in Early Modern England

*Ongoing research*

This strand of my doctoral research compares private charitable giving and publicly administered parish relief. It examines differences in the financing, allocation, and targeting of assistance and considers how local institutions may have shaped their development.
