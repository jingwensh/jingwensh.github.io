My research draws on annual poor-relief expenditure data from **312 parishes across 42 English counties**, combining newly collected archival records with the parish series assembled by [Brodie Waddell (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1093/pastj/gtaa027). I have also digitised **approximately 167k individual disbursement entries** from parish accounts.

<p class="research-status">All papers listed below are works in progress.</p>

<div class="research-list">
  <section class="research-entry">
    <h2>Why Did Poor Relief Expand?</h2>
    <p>How did England develop such an extensive system of publicly financed poor relief, and what drove its long-run growth? The paper asks how temporary subsistence pressures, alongside local fiscal responses, contributed to the evolution of relief provision.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-entry">
    <h2>Did Poor Relief Supplement Wages before Speenhamland?</h2>
    <p>Did parish poor relief function partly as wage supplementation before Speenhamland? This paper asks whether relief responded differently to food-price shocks when wages were weak, distinguishing potential wage support from more general responses to subsistence pressure.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-entry">
    <h2>Selective Welfare in Early Modern England: Poor Relief, Charity, and Regional Divergence, 1600–1786</h2>
    <p>How did assistance become more selective as welfare provision expanded? This paper traces changes in <strong>who received support</strong> and <strong>the purposes for which it was provided</strong>, comparing the evolution of public poor relief and private charity across regions.</p>
  </section>
</div>
